Microscope vs flow cytometer

Same cells, two ways of looking

A microscope gives you a picture: you can see each cell's shape and where the glowing tags sit, but someone has to look at cells one by one. A flow cytometer never makes a picture. It measures how big, how grainy and how bright each cell is as it zooms past the laser, so it can check thousands of cells every second and count each type.

MicroscopeFlow cytometer
What you getA picture of each cellNumbers for each cell, shown as dots on a graph
Cells areStill, stuck on a glass slideFloating in liquid, flowing single file
SpeedTens to hundreds of cellsThousands of cells every second
Best forSeeing shape, and where things are inside the cellCounting how many cells of each type are in a sample
