Fragmenting the DNA

Genomic DNA is far longer than any single read. Before it goes anywhere near the flow cell, every molecule in the sample is sheared into short pieces at random points, all at once — this is happening across millions of molecules in parallel, not one at a time.
Intact genomic DNA
A fragment here is roughly 200–300 base pairs. The smallest human chromosome is around 46 million base pairs — so a single fragment is a sliver, on the order of 1/150,000th of one chromosome.
